Your client says they paid you USD 1,000. A few days later the RMB on your card, converted back, is clearly a chunk short. The money isn't lost. It's been split into a few small pieces left at points along the way. PayPal took one when you received, a little more went when it became RMB, and another piece went at withdrawal or settlement. Write the pieces out one by one and you won't be puzzled again.

<mark><strong>Count fees leg by leg: once when you receive, once when you convert, once more when you withdraw or settle, and maybe once at the bank. Write each leg as a formula, with the date you checked the fee page next to it.</strong></mark>

Every number below says "as of 2026-11-19," the date we checked these fee pages, and where a page shows its own last-updated date, that's given too. Fees can change at any time, so before you use a number, reopen each page from the list in the last section.

## Meet the four legs

- **Receiving fee.** Taken when money enters your account, such as PayPal's percentage plus a fixed amount.
- **<Term k="换汇费" />.** Charged when USD becomes RMB. It often isn't listed separately but sits inside the exchange rate. PayPal's fee centre says the conversion fee in cross-border collection is "included in the dynamically adjusted transaction exchange rate" ([PayPal China fee centre](https://www.paypal.cn/portal/fees?locale.x=zh)). So it doesn't show up as a deduction. It shows up in the rate you get.
- **Withdrawal or settlement fee.** Taken when money leaves the receiving account, such as a fixed amount per PayPal wire or a percentage settlement fee for cross-border collection.
- **Bank charges.** The banks a wire passes through, and your own bank, may each take something. This leg is the hardest to look up. We found no official figure for what Chinese banks charge to receive a transfer from abroad, so your bank is the one to tell you.

## 贝宝支付 receiving, then cross-border collection into RMB

As of 2026-11-19, each leg on this route works like this:

1. **Receiving fee** = payment amount × 4.40% + USD 0.30. That's the rate on PayPal's merchant fee page for international payments when the recipient is in mainland China, and the page says it applies to accounts opened at 贝宝支付 under the cross-border agreement ([PayPal merchant fees, last updated 2025-06-18](https://www.paypal.com/c2/business/paypal-business-fees?locale.x=zh_C2)).
2. **RMB settlement fee** = amount settled × 1.0%, the standard rate. From 15 January to 31 December 2026 (Beijing time) there's a limited-time offer, which the page writes as "0.5%封顶" (0.5%, capped), for cross-border collections made from a USD balance only. From 1 to 14 January the standard rate applied ([PayPal China fee centre](https://www.paypal.cn/portal/fees?locale.x=zh)).
3. **VAT** = RMB settlement fee × 6%. The fee centre says the settlement fee doesn't include VAT, and VAT at 6% of the fee is charged when the fee is.
4. **Conversion fee.** USD or HKD into RMB is 0% during the offer (15 January to 31 December 2026), with a standard rate of 0.2%, and other currencies into RMB are 2.5%. PayPal's service partner outside China charges it, and it's built into the transaction rate shown on each request rather than deducted separately.

Whether "0.5%封顶" means a flat 0.5% or tiered pricing that tops out at 0.5% is only shown on an offer page behind a login, and we can't confirm it. So the example below uses the standard 1.0%. You may be charged less, and what the page shows when you make the request is what counts.

Take a payment of USD 1,000 at the standard rates:

- Receiving fee = 1,000 × 4.40% + 0.30 = USD 44.30, leaving USD 955.70 in the account.
- Settling all of it, the settlement fee = 955.70 × 1.0% ≈ USD 9.56 equivalent.
- VAT = 9.56 × 6% ≈ USD 0.57 equivalent.
- That leaves roughly the equivalent of USD 945.57, which times PayPal's transaction rate at the time is the RMB on your card. The fee page doesn't say which currency the settlement fee and VAT come out of, so this is a rough sum in USD terms.

For a USD 100 payment worked through step by step, see the how-to [What a USD 100 PayPal payment leaves you with](/en/howto/paypal-100/).

## A PayPal USD wire home, then settling at the bank

On this route, a 贝宝支付 account receives the money and, instead of using cross-border collection, wires the USD to your own bank account in China. As of 2026-11-19:

1. **Receiving fee**: the same as above, payment amount × 4.40% + USD 0.30.
2. **Wire withdrawal fee** = USD 35 per withdrawal to a bank account in mainland China. Both the merchant fee page that applies to 贝宝支付 accounts (last updated 2025-06-18) and the older accounts' personal fee page ([PayPal consumer fees, last updated 2021-07-30](https://www.paypal.com/c2/webapps/mpp/paypal-fees?locale.x=zh_C2)) give this figure. The minimum withdrawal is USD 150 ([PayPal help page](https://www.paypal.com/c2/cshelp/article/%E5%A6%82%E4%BD%95%E4%BB%8E%E6%88%91%E7%9A%84paypal%E8%B4%A6%E6%88%B7%E6%8F%90%E5%8F%96%E8%B5%84%E9%87%91%EF%BC%9F-help394)). If a wire comes back because the details were wrong, the merchant fee page lists USD 15.
3. **Conversion fee**: withdrawing USD from a USD balance involves no currency conversion. Withdrawing in a currency other than your balance's, the fee page says, adds a conversion fee.
4. **The bank's side**: whether the banks along the way or your own bank take anything, PayPal's pages don't say, so ask your bank. Once it's at your bank, you settle at the bank's real-time quoted rate ([Bank of China: personal settlement](https://www.boc.cn/pbservice/pb4/200806/t20080626_758.html)).

Back to the USD 1,000. After the receiving fee you have USD 955.70, the wire takes USD 35, and USD 920.70 is left before any bank-side charges, which you then settle at the bank's rate. The USD 35 is fixed, so on the same route it's a very different share of a USD 150 withdrawal than of a USD 5,000 one.

## Wise receiving USD, then Swift home

1. **Receiving fee**: as of 2026-11-19, a client in the US paying by ACH is free, a US domestic wire is USD 6.11, and a Swift payment from outside the US is USD 6.11. Wise also notes that correspondent banks on a Swift route might apply their own fees ([Wise: receiving with USD account details](https://wise.com/help/articles/2827506/how-do-i-receive-money-with-my-usd-account-details), no page date shown).
2. **Sending USD to China** = Swift fee + Wise fee. As of 2026-11-19, Wise lists China among the countries where correspondent fees are predicted, with a Swift fee of USD 7.65 per transfer. Its example is a recipient in China receiving USD 4,000, for USD 8.78 in total, made up of the USD 7.65 Swift fee and a USD 1.13 Wise fee. Wise says it predicts correspondent fees up front and adds them to your fee so the recipient gets the full amount ([Wise: sending USD outside the US](https://wise.com/help/articles/2946451/how-much-does-it-cost-to-send-usd-to-countries-outside-the-us), no page date shown). For the Wise fee on your own transfer, go by the transfer screen.
3. **Conversion fee**: sending USD from a USD balance means no conversion on Wise's side. Once it reaches your bank, you settle at the bank's quoted rate.
4. **The bank's side**: whether your bank charges to receive the transfer, ask your bank.

For how to do it, see the how-to [Move USD from Wise to your Chinese bank card](/en/howto/wise-to-bank/).

## Payoneer and WorldFirst

**Payoneer.** As of 2026-11-19, its pricing page (last updated 2026-01-01) says:

1. **Receiving fee**: free into a receiving account in your local currency, 1% with a minimum of USD 1.00 into a receiving account in a non-local currency, up to 3.99% + USD 0.49 when the payer uses a credit card, and varying by marketplace for marketplace payouts.
2. **Withdrawal fee**: 1.2% to 4% to a bank account when currency conversion is involved.
3. **Annual fee**: USD 29.95 a year if the account receives less than USD 6,000 in any 12 consecutive months.

The pricing page itself says these are estimates based on the most common fee structure, that fees can differ by territory and account type, and that the fees that apply to you are shown at registration and any time under Fees in your account ([Payoneer pricing](https://www.payoneer.com/pricing/)). What it charges now for withdrawals to mainland Chinese banks isn't stated on its pages, so go by the Fees page in your account.

**WorldFirst.** As of 2026-11-19, its pricing page lists a 0.3% rate for new users and says its exchange rate tracks the market rate, with no page date shown ([WorldFirst pricing](https://www.worldfirst.com.cn/cn/pricing/)). There's no full public fee table beyond the 0.3%, so ask a WorldFirst account manager before you open an account.

## Why we don't give a total percentage

You may want one number, like "PayPal takes X% all in." We don't give one, because no such number exists. The USD 0.30 fixed receiving fee, the USD 35 wire fee and the USD 7.65 Swift fee are all fixed, so the smaller the payment, the bigger their share. Exchange rates move every day, and the conversion fee is hidden inside the rate. On the same route, a different amount or a different day gives a different percentage. So this site gives formulas only. Put your own amounts in, and what comes out is yours.

## Stuck?

PayPal shows you the rate each time you make a cross-border collection request, so screenshot that page and compare it with what arrived. If it still doesn't add up, take the screenshot to the provider's support.</p>
</Q>

</Q>
