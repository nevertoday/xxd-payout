It's November. You check your mobile banking and see you've settled nearly USD 50,000 this year, and two clients still owe you final payments. A small panic sets in: does that mean you can't change the rest? When you finish this page, you'll know how to settle the part above the quota at your bank, which documents to take, and who gets the final say.

<mark>USD 50,000 is a facilitation quota, not a ceiling. If your real income goes over it, take your ID and the documents for that income to the bank and settle it as usual. Which documents it needs is up to the bank handling it.</mark>

## What the USD 50,000 actually covers

<Term k="结汇" /> means turning foreign currency you've received into RMB. SAFE's rules give individuals one <Term k="年度便利化额度" /> for settlement and a separate one for buying foreign currency, each worth USD 50,000 per person per year ([Implementing Rules](http://m.safe.gov.cn/safe/2007/0105/5320.html), Article 2; [Current Account FX Guidelines](http://m.safe.gov.cn/safe/file/file/20200831/f0127756271c43ef9b3908d5d157bd91.pdf), Article 54). Within the quota, your own valid ID is all you need, at the counter or in online or mobile banking (Guidelines, Article 55).

The word "facilitation" is the key. SAFE's Xiamen branch answers this exact question: real, lawful <Term k="经常项目" /> settlements and purchases have no amount limit, and the USD 50,000 is an annual facilitation quota, not a yearly limit on what a person can settle or buy ([SAFE Xiamen](https://www.safe.gov.cn/xiamen/file/file/20211022/f4794075e84e40dd9394be523a52731b.pdf)). Put another way, within the quota the bank only looks at your ID. Above it, the bank looks at one more thing: where the money really came from.

The quota runs by calendar year, 1 January to 31 December. Whatever you don't use is cleared at year end and doesn't carry over. You can check what's left in mobile or online banking, or take your ID to any bank branch that handles FX settlement and ask ([SAFE Xiamen](https://www.safe.gov.cn/xiamen/file/file/20211022/f4794075e84e40dd9394be523a52731b.pdf)).

## For the part above it, bring your ID and transaction documents

Article 56 of the Guidelines says a domestic individual can settle current account income at a bank without using the annual quota by showing their own valid ID and documents of where the money came from, with the transaction amounts on them ([Guidelines](http://m.safe.gov.cn/safe/file/file/20200831/f0127756271c43ef9b3908d5d157bd91.pdf)). So a settlement you make with documents doesn't come out of the USD 50,000 in the first place, and it works whether or not you have quota left.

Article 10 of the Implementing Rules lists which documents go with which kind of income. The lines closest to freelance work and creator income are these ([Implementing Rules](http://m.safe.gov.cn/safe/2007/0105/5320.html), Article 10):

- Royalties and licence income: proof of payment, and an agreement or contract.
- Income from legal, accounting, consulting and public relations services: proof of payment, and an agreement or contract.
- Employee pay: the employment contract and proof of income.
- Other: relevant proof and payment records.

Bank of China's personal settlement page reproduces the same table for settlements above the quota ([BOC personal settlement](https://www.boc.cn/pbservice/pb4/200806/t20080626_758.html)). You'll notice nearly every line comes down to two things: an agreement or contract that says why you're being paid, and a payment record that shows the money really arrived. That's why it pays to keep both from your very first job, so you aren't digging for them at year end. [Keep the paper trail from day one](/en/guide/3-paper-trail/) goes into this.

## Which category, which documents: the bank decides

You might wonder whether your design work counts as "consulting services" or "other". This site won't pick a category for you. Article 68 of the Guidelines says the bank must reasonably check that the transaction documents are genuine and match the FX payment. The bank can decide, based on your risk profile, which documents it reviews, in what form and what it looks for, and it can refuse a transaction whose authenticity is in doubt. If it refuses, it has to tell you why and how to appeal ([Guidelines](http://m.safe.gov.cn/safe/file/file/20200831/f0127756271c43ef9b3908d5d157bd91.pdf), Article 68).

So the easiest thing is to call your branch before you go and ask which documents to bring for this income. At the counter, just explain it as it is. In English, it might go like this: "I take on design work from clients abroad through online platforms, and the service fees I've received this year are now over USD 50,000. This payment is a service fee from a client, and I've brought the contract and the payment record. I'd like to settle it to RMB. Is there anything else you need from me?" <Callout type="no" title="Don't break a payment into smaller ones, and don't borrow anyone's quota">
The rules are blunt about this. Individuals may not get around the limits by splitting amounts or any similar method, and may not use false commercial documents or records to get around authenticity checks ([Personal FX Measures](https://www.safe.gov.cn/tianjin/2024/0430/2465.html), Article 7). Anyone who uses another person's facilitation quota goes straight onto SAFE's <Term k="关注名单" />, and for that year and the next two, every settlement or purchase needs transaction documents ([Guidelines](http://m.safe.gov.cn/safe/file/file/20200831/f0127756271c43ef9b3908d5d157bd91.pdf), Article 62). Real income can be settled with documents anyway, so there's nothing to get around.
</Callout>

## If 贝宝支付 settled the money to RMB for you

If you use a PayPal account at 贝宝支付, the money is settled to RMB inside PayPal and paid to your card, which is a different situation. SAFE's Guidelines say personal FX settlement under a <Term k="支付机构" />'s FX business doesn't count toward the personal annual quota ([Guidelines](http://m.safe.gov.cn/safe/file/file/20200831/f0127756271c43ef9b3908d5d157bd91.pdf), Article 137). But PayPal's pages only say the conversion is done by its service partner outside China, and never say which kind of business its RMB settlement is ([fee centre](https://www.paypal.cn/portal/fees?locale.x=zh)). PayPal's pages don't spell this out, so ask PayPal China before you rely on it, ideally in writing.

Whether this income is taxed, and how, is a tax question. This site doesn't cover tax, so ask your local tax authority or a professional.

## Stuck?

<Q q="The bank says my documents aren't enough and won't settle it.">
Under Article 68, a bank that refuses has to tell you why and how to appeal. Ask exactly which documents it needs, and in what form, and go back once you have them.
</Q>
<Q q="I don't have a formal contract, only platform orders and payment records.">
The bank decides on documents, and the "other" line in Article 10 of the Implementing Rules asks for relevant proof and payment records. Take whatever you have that shows this transaction, and ask your branch first whether it will accept it.
</Q>
<Q q="Can I settle with documents mid-year and keep my USD 50,000 for later?">
Yes. Article 56 of the Guidelines is about exactly that, settling with your ID and transaction documents without using the quota, and it doesn't require you to use up the quota first.
</Q>
<Q q="Can I carry this year's unused quota into next year?">
No. SAFE Xiamen says unused quota is cleared automatically at the end of the calendar year, and you get a fresh one the next year.
</Q>

Now you know: within the quota, your ID is enough; above it, your ID plus transaction documents, and the bank decides which documents. If the money is still sitting in Wise, start with [Move USD from Wise to your Chinese bank card](/en/howto/wise-to-bank/).
